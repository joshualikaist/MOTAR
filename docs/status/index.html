How do perception, temporal state and local geometry change the behavior of a frozen UAV policy in clutter? MOTAR measures those effects—and studies when lost-target tracking should give way to active reacquisition.

01 / System

A measured loop, from sensing to motion.

A forward RGB-D camera and LiDAR support target tracking and obstacle representation. A frozen policy requests planar motion and yaw; the task retains altitude hold, and a configured speed cap precedes low-level control.

Target tracking and obstacle sensing overlap.

Camera measurements initialize target tracking; LiDAR can assist association and range updates. LiDAR and depth also support obstacle features. Own state comes from the simulator, rather than a qualified hardware estimator.

Ground truth has a defined boundary.

Scene state generates synthetic sensor observations and supplies rewards, termination, evaluation and the critic. It is not injected directly as target pose, semantic mask or visibility into the actor observation.

03 / Recorded evidence

Motion changes more than difficulty.

Against the motion lineage used during training, a static target reduced capture while obstacle-aware target motion improved the aggregate result. Seven new seeds independently replicated all three directional contrasts under a corrected per-environment quota.

Policy F · 7 independent evaluation seeds · four densities · 229,376 primary episodes. pp = percentage points. All three Holm-adjusted p-values are 0.046875, at the resolution boundary of this seven-seed exact sign-flip design.

The E2 gain is not uniform at high density. At 205 bars, four seed differences are positive, two negative and one zero. No target law reacts to the pursuer; obstacle-aware is not adversarial. 04 / Sensitivity studies

Different interventions. Different evidence.

Measured perception error, readaptation, filter geometry and rendering were investigated in separate experiments. Their effects retain their own checkpoint, replication unit and uncertainty interval.

Perception cost is clear; readaptation is inconclusive.

The measured-error injector cost Frozen F −4.57 pp capture. The readaptation estimate was +0.73 pp and its seed-level interval spans zero. Real-footage error measurement remains a separate offline lineage.

Geometry changed crash rate, not the safety contract.

Arc-clearance versus riskcap produced a seed-mean crash difference of −1.4942 pp, seed-t 95% CI [−2.42, −0.57]. The historical pooled estimate is −1.4903 pp. A lower configured crash rate is not a safety guarantee.

Rendering sensitivity belongs to Policy R.

The −48.967 pp capture loss is MATERIAL_LOSS. Policy R is a different checkpoint from F; renderer-defect causality is NOT_TESTED.

07 / Reproducibility

Results stay attached to their contracts.

The historical experiment records, preregistrations, checkpoints and original figure packages remain unchanged. This page redraws the presentation; it does not run a new experiment.

What can be inspected

Evidence ledger, figure data and hashes, source verification and corrections, and the pinned BOIA progress record. Public code continues to point to the separate MOTAR-public release; the local BOIA implementation is not published by this page.

What remains outside the evidence

No real-flight validation, sim-to-real transfer, calibrated identity, BOIA capture benefit or closed-loop safety is established. Minimum-distance results remain WITHHELD_SEMANTIC_MISMATCH. Raw replication evidence is retained outside normal Git history and is not publicly downloadable.
